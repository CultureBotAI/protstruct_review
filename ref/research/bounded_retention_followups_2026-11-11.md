## `#1050` — Saved Pre-Execution Checkpoint Leaf Nonblocking Open, Pre-Read Observed Size Consistency, and Final Relative-Name Binding (`pre_execution_checkpoint_baseline`)

### Scope and Motivation

In `scripts/onesar_refmac.py` (`_verify_immutable_baselines_post_run`), post-run verification of the saved pre-execution checkpoint (`refmac_pre_execution_checkpoint.json` under `pre_execution_checkpoint_baseline`) already admitted the parent directory capability (`cp_parent_cap`), performed an initial parent-descriptor-relative `os.stat(cp_leaf_name, dir_fd=cp_parent_cap.fd, follow_symlinks=False)`, opened the relative leaf entry with `O_RDONLY | O_NOFOLLOW | O_CLOEXEC`, and checked pre-read and post-EOF `os.fstat(cp_fd)` before re-verifying the ancestor chain and admitted parent descriptor.

Three narrow leaf-readback gaps remained on the saved checkpoint path:

1. **Blocking open on a regular-to-FIFO swap between initial `stat` and `open`**: if the relative checkpoint leaf entry was replaced by a named pipe (FIFO) after the initial `os.stat` check and before `os.open`, opening without `O_NONBLOCK` could block indefinitely before `leaf_pre_read_fstat` (`os.fstat(cp_fd)`) could reject the nonregular descriptor.
2. **Pre-read descriptor `fstat` byte-size contradiction against initial relative `stat` (`#1050`, comment `6788347943`)**: if the initial relative `os.stat` observed byte size `cp_initial_size` (for example, `26` bytes), the same regular `(dev, ino)` grew or shrank before `os.fstat(cp_fd)` (for example, `cp_pre_fstat_size == 54`), and the original `26`-byte payload was restored on that same inode before `os.read(cp_fd, ...)`, `leaf_pre_read_fstat` previously checked only `stat.S_ISREG(cp_fd_st.st_mode)` and `(cp_pre_fstat_dev, cp_pre_fstat_ino) == (cp_initial_dev, cp_initial_ino)` without requiring `cp_pre_fstat_size == cp_initial_size`, allowing `leaf_pre_fstat_verified = True` and `matches_baseline = True` despite a recorded `26` vs `54` byte contradiction in the retained audit record.
3. **Post-EOF relative directory-entry rebinding before leaf close**: if the relative checkpoint entry in `cp_parent_cap.fd` was unlinked or replaced (for example, by a new regular inode containing identical bytes, a different-size regular file, a symlink, a FIFO, or a directory) while `cp_fd` was open and being read to EOF, `os.fstat(cp_fd)` alone only inspected the already-opened descriptor and did not verify that the relative leaf name under `cp_parent_cap.fd` still bound to that same regular `(dev, ino)` and byte size at EOF.

### Implemented Contract in `scripts/onesar_refmac.py`

Within the saved checkpoint leaf verification block of `_verify_immutable_baselines_post_run`:

- **Nonblocking relative open**: `os.open(cp_leaf_name, ..., dir_fd=cp_parent_cap.fd)` includes `getattr(os, "O_NONBLOCK", 0)` alongside `os.O_RDONLY | getattr(os, "O_NOFOLLOW", 0) | getattr(os, "O_CLOEXEC", 0)`.
- **Pre-read and post-EOF descriptor `fstat` checks**:
  - `leaf_pre_read_fstat` verifies `stat.S_ISREG(cp_fd_st.st_mode)`, `(cp_pre_fstat_dev, cp_pre_fstat_ino) == (cp_initial_dev, cp_initial_ino)`, and `cp_pre_fstat_size == cp_initial_size` before setting `leaf_pre_fstat_verified = True` and before any `os.read(cp_fd, ...)` call. On any mismatch, `leaf_pre_fstat_verified` remains `False` and `OSError(errno.ESTALE, "Pre-execution checkpoint inode, file type, or size changed while opening descriptor: expected regular file (dev=..., ino=..., size=...), observed (regular=..., dev=..., ino=..., size=...)", str(cp_path))` is raised under `failure_phase = "leaf_pre_read_fstat"`.
  - `leaf_read` reads chunks until actual EOF (`chunk == b""`).
  - `leaf_post_read_fstat` verifies `stat.S_ISREG(cp_post_st.st_mode)`, `(cp_post_fstat_dev, cp_post_fstat_ino) == (cp_initial_dev, cp_initial_ino)`, `cp_post_fstat_size == cp_pre_fstat_size` (which already equals `cp_initial_size`), and `cp_post_fstat_size == len(cp_bytes)`. On any mismatch, `OSError(errno.ESTALE, "Pre-execution checkpoint inode, file type, or size changed while reading descriptor: expected regular file (dev=..., ino=..., initial_size=..., pre_read_size=...), observed (regular=..., dev=..., ino=..., read_bytes=..., size=...)", str(cp_path))` is raised under `failure_phase = "leaf_post_read_fstat"`.
- **Final admitted-parent-relative `os.stat` before leaf close (`leaf_post_read_stat`)**:
  - After `leaf_post_read_fstat` succeeds and before `os.close(cp_fd)` (inside the owned `try ... finally` cleanup envelope for `cp_fd`), `_verify_immutable_baselines_post_run` executes `os.stat(cp_leaf_name, dir_fd=cp_parent_cap.fd, follow_symlinks=False)`.
  - If `os.stat` raises `OSError`, the original syscall exception is preserved unchanged as the primary checkpoint failure (`failure_phase = "leaf_post_read_stat"`). On `FileNotFoundError` / `errno.ENOENT`, `final_leaf_lookup` records `exists=False`, `is_symlink=False`, `is_regular_file=False`, and `None` for identity/size/mode; on other `OSError`s (such as `EIO` or `EACCES`), unknown final type/identity/size/mode fields remain `None`.
  - If `os.stat` succeeds, the final lookup must be a regular non-symlink (`not stat.S_ISLNK(st_mode)` and `stat.S_ISREG(st_mode)`), match the opened descriptor's `(dev, ino)` (`(cp_post_fstat_dev, cp_post_fstat_ino)`), and match both `cp_post_fstat_size` and `len(cp_bytes)`. Otherwise `OSError(errno.ESTALE, ..., str(cp_path))` is raised under `failure_phase = "leaf_post_read_stat"`.
- **Separated retention of initial lookup, descriptor `fstat`, and final relative-name `stat` (`#1255`)**:
  - Once both the initial relative `os.stat` lookup and its immediate post-stat ancestor/parent re-verification succeed (covering `leaf_type_check`, leaf open, `leaf_pre_read_fstat`, `leaf_read`, `leaf_post_read_fstat`, `leaf_post_read_stat`, and subsequent post-readback branches; the earlier `post_leaf_stat_ancestor_reverify` refusal branch retains its older pre-leaf-check shape without structured `initial_leaf_*` / `initial_leaf_lookup` fields), `stat_size_bytes`, `initial_leaf_size_bytes`, and `initial_leaf_lookup["size_bytes"]` (along with `initial_leaf_lookup`, `initial_leaf_dev`, `initial_leaf_ino`, and `initial_leaf_mode`) always retain the initial lookup values (`cp_initial_size`, `cp_initial_dev`, `cp_initial_ino`, `cp_initial_mode`) across all subsequent branches without being overwritten when descriptor `fstat` or final `stat` differs or fails.
  - Top-level `size_bytes` is branch-dependent by design in `_verify_immutable_baselines_post_run`: on early `leaf_type_check` refusal and on leaf open / `leaf_pre_read_fstat` / `leaf_read` / `leaf_post_read_fstat` / `leaf_post_read_stat` `OSError` failures, `size_bytes` records the initial lookup size (`cp_initial_size`); after completed leaf readback and final relative `os.stat` verification (including later `post_leaf_read_ancestor_reverify` refusal as well as final baseline comparison), `size_bytes` records `cp_size` (`len(cp_bytes)`, the captured byte count from readback).
  - Descriptor observations are retained in `leaf_descriptor_opened`, `leaf_pre_fstat_verified`, `leaf_pre_fstat_is_regular_file`, `leaf_pre_fstat_dev`, `leaf_pre_fstat_ino`, `leaf_pre_fstat_size_bytes`, `leaf_post_fstat_verified`, `leaf_post_fstat_is_regular_file`, `leaf_post_fstat_dev`, `leaf_post_fstat_ino`, and `leaf_post_fstat_size_bytes`.
  - Final relative-name observations are retained in `final_leaf_lookup` and flat `leaf_final_stat_*` fields (`attempted`, `succeeded`, `verified`, `exists`, `is_symlink`, `is_regular_file`, `dev`, `ino`, `size_bytes`, `mode`).
  - On any `leaf_pre_read_fstat` or `leaf_post_read_stat` failure, accepted `sha256` remains `None` and `matches_baseline` remains `False`, while observed stream/descriptor facts, owned leaf/parent close results (`cleanup_close_error`, `parent_close_error`), and cause sinks (`caught_exceptions`, `pre_checkpoint_cause_out`, `mutation_causes_out`) are preserved in chronological order.

## `#1168` — Post-EOF Failed-Stage and Controlled-Root Partial-Member Observation Retention in `scripts/refmac_diagnostic_profile.py`

### Scope and Motivation

In `scripts/refmac_diagnostic_profile.py`, two observation-retention gaps affected regular-file and controlled-root verification:

1. **Post-EOF verification and failed-stage sub-observations in `_stream_observe_regular_file`**:
   Once streaming read reached actual EOF (`chunk == b""`), `_stream_observe_regular_file` previously raised plain `ValueError` (or lost intermediate sub-stage measurements) when verification failed at or after `end_fstat`:
   - `end_fstat` (`os.fstat(handle.fileno())`),
   - `descriptor_identity_comparison` (`_stat_identity_tuple(fstat_after) != _stat_identity_tuple(fstat_before)`),
   - `descriptor_size_comparison` (`total_bytes != int(fstat_before.st_size) or total_bytes != int(fstat_after.st_size)`),
   - `final_kernel_lookup` (`_validate_kernel_regular_lookup(raw_lexical)`),
   - `final_path_traversal` (`_traverse_path_components(raw_lexical)`),
   - `final_target_resolution` (`_resolve_regular_target_strict(raw_lexical)`), and
   - `post_read_namespace_comparison` (post-read comparisons of `lex_lstat_after`, `symlinks_after`, `traversal_after.directory_identities`, `resolved_after`, `target_lstat_after`, and `lex_stat_after`).
   In addition, when post-EOF `_validate_kernel_regular_lookup`, `_traverse_path_components`, or `_resolve_regular_target_strict` failed mid-helper (for example, when the lexical or resolved entry was replaced by a directory, FIFO, or symlink, when `os.readlink` failed or returned a directory-suffixed target, when a revisited traversal component changed target or inode, or when a later ancestor `os.lstat` failed after the initial resolved-target `os.lstat` succeeded), the helper's already-measured nonregular/component `stat`/`lstat`/`readlink` facts and failure sub-stage needed to be retained without performing a second filesystem retry.
2. **Controlled-root inventory partial-member retention in `_observe_controlled_root`**:
   When `_observe_regular_file_pin` raised `_PartialFileReadError` during controlled-root enumeration (`_observe_controlled_root`), `_ControlledRootObservationError` carried the `_PartialFileReadError` in `causes` during admission, but `_ObservedRootInventory` and `RootObservation` had no root-local field to retain the failed member's `PartialFileReadObservation`, and `_reobserve_outer_profile_state` / `recheck_profile` dropped the partial carrier while keeping only the error string and underlying cause exception. Because a root member can be observed both during uncontrolled-size root inventory and later as a separate declared-file pin, failed inventory reads must be retained under the owning root's relative member key separately from `files` / `partial_files`.

## `#1045` — Retained JSON Temporary Entries, Explicit Temporary-Handling Disposition Contract, and Early-Finalization Cause Preservation (`scripts/onesar_refmac.py`)

### Scope and Motivation

In `scripts/onesar_refmac.py`, `_atomic_write_new_json_file` stages each JSON write in a target-derived exclusive temporary file (`f".{normalized_target.name}.tmp.{os.getpid()}"`) inside the admitted parent directory (`2727`). Because POSIX `unlinkat` acts on the relative directory-entry name without an atomic `(st_dev, st_ino)` precondition, unlinking the temporary entry after a pre-unlink `os.stat` left a narrow check-to-unlink kernel interval on `tmp_name`. Issue `#1045` eliminates temporary unlinking from `_atomic_write_new_json_file` and records an explicit retained-temporary handling contract across `_atomic_write_new_json_file`, `_write_json_with_sibling_collision_fallback`, and the collector persistence consumers.

### Implemented Contract in `scripts/onesar_refmac.py`

- **Policy, dispositions, and satisfaction classifiers (`2325–2654`)**:
  - The only recognized explicit policy is `JSON_TEMP_HANDLING_POLICY_RETAIN_V1 = "retain_entries_v1"` in `RECOGNIZED_JSON_TEMP_HANDLING_POLICIES` (`2325–2328`); informal prose strings (`"retain_temporaries"`, `"unlink_temporaries"`, `"retain_temporaries:v1"`) are rejected. Historical receipts without `_JSON_TEMP_POLICY_MARKER_KEYS` (`"temp_handling_policy"`, `"temp_handling_succeeded"`, `"temp_handling_disposition"`) are handled by the separate marker-absent compatibility branches in `_is_json_temp_status_handling_satisfied` (`2503–2541`) and `_is_json_temp_handling_satisfied` (`2636–2654`) rather than an explicit legacy policy identifier.
  - New writer receipts carry `temp_handling_policy`, `temp_handling_succeeded`, `temp_handling_disposition`, and `temp_cleanup_status` (alongside truthful legacy `temp_cleanup_succeeded = False`). `temp_cleanup_status` records `temp_observation_phase`, `observed_temp_present`, `observed_temp_is_regular_file`, `observed_temp_is_symlink`, `observed_temp_mode`, `expected_temp_dev`, `expected_temp_ino`, `observed_temp_dev`, `observed_temp_ino`, and truthful legacy `unlinked = False`, `unlink_attempted = False`, `unlink_attempt_count = 0`, and `cleanup_verified = False` (`3053–3104`). Exceptions receive nested `temp_cleanup_status`, actual cleanup-error metadata and, when available, a `published_receipt` through `_attach_cleanup_metadata_to_exception`; this does not promise top-level policy fields on every exception.
  - Recognized dispositions in `VALID_JSON_TEMP_HANDLING_DISPOSITIONS` (`2329–2346`) are `JSON_TEMP_DISPOSITION_RETAINED = "owned_temp_retained_by_policy"`, `JSON_TEMP_DISPOSITION_IDENTITY_MISMATCH = "temp_path_identity_mismatch"`, `JSON_TEMP_DISPOSITION_ALREADY_ABSENT = "temp_path_already_absent"`, `JSON_TEMP_DISPOSITION_IDENTITY_UNKNOWN = "initial_temp_fstat_identity_unknown"`, `JSON_TEMP_DISPOSITION_STAT_ERROR = "temp_observation_stat_error"`, and `JSON_TEMP_DISPOSITION_NOT_CREATED = "temp_not_created"`.
  - Real successful retention (`temp_handling_succeeded is True`, `temp_handling_disposition == "owned_temp_retained_by_policy"`) is distinct from the truthful legacy `unlinked`/`cleanup_verified` `False` flags, whereas any `False` or unknown handling state fails strict operational classification in `_is_json_temp_status_handling_satisfied` and `_is_json_temp_handling_satisfied` (`_is_json_temp_handling_satisfied` also accepts nested-only or historical shapes per `2553–2654` without requiring every top-level field on every historical/nested-only mapping).
  - When initial `os.fstat(fd)` fails before `(tmp_dev, tmp_ino)` is known, `temp_handling_disposition` is `"initial_temp_fstat_identity_unknown"` with `temp_observation_phase = None`, `observed_temp_present = None`, and `temp_present_after_cleanup = None` (leaving presence unknown rather than proved present). Actual `OSError` objects from temporary `os.stat` failures are retained in `cleanup_errors` / `temp_cleanup_errors` / `temp_cleanup_error`, while absence, identity-mismatch, or unknown-identity anomalies are recorded in `temp_cleanup_status` without synthesizing a custom cleanup exception class.
- **Observation phases and point-in-time scope (`2899–3120`, `3381–3422`)**:
  - No `os.unlink` call occurs in `_atomic_write_new_json_file`. Immediately after `os.link` succeeds and **before** target readback (`3381–3422`), the writer performs the `"post_link_stat"` check via `os.stat(tmp_name, dir_fd=parent_fd, follow_symlinks=False)`.
  - `_finalize_temp_cleanup()` (which takes no arguments, `2899–3120`) reuses an already verified post-link observation (`post_link_temp_verified and not cleanup_errors`, `2950–2961`) rather than performing a new `os.stat` after every later readback, namespace, or close failure; when `created_tmp` is `True` and post-link verification did not already succeed (and initial identity is known without a prior post-link absence/mismatch), `_finalize_temp_cleanup()` performs `"final_cleanup_stat"` (`2962–3038`), preserving actual `OSError`s and nullable unknowns. These descriptor-relative `os.stat` checks prove only the point-in-time directory-entry state observed at that syscall.
- **Hardlink aliases, unpublished temporary content (`#1272`), scanner behavior, and storage cost**:
  - When `os.link` succeeds, the retained `.{name}.tmp.{pid}` entry and the canonical target are hardlink aliases sharing the same inode and bytes on disk (not independent copies). When an attempt fails before publication or loses an `os.link` collision, its retained temporary file holds whatever bytes were written (`0`, partial, or complete valid JSON, as in `fsync` failures, payload-factory temporary swaps, or losing collisions) with no universal presence, link-count, or empty-content guarantee after arbitrary namespace changes; JSON parseability never makes a temporary file an authoritative publication.
  - `_scan_refmac_directory_entries` (`12302–12328`) inspects entries present in `refmac_dir` at scan time by reopening each path through `_inspect_output_artifact` and records non-canonical entries (including any retained `.{name}.tmp.{pid}` files already present when `_scan_refmac_directory_entries` runs) in the filename-keyed `additional_refmac_dir_files` mapping; files created after the scan (such as the final collector manifest or post-scan fallback artifacts) cannot appear in that earlier scan.
  - Retained temporaries carry on-disk directory-entry and byte storage cost (published aliases share an inode; losing or unpublished temporaries retain their written bytes). Sibling and fallback-directory retry loops are bounded by `MAX_SIBLING_COLLISION_ATTEMPTS = 64` and `MAX_FALLBACK_DIR_ATTEMPTS = 64`, which bound per-call retry attempts rather than providing a global storage cap or competing-writer exclusion; outcome fingerprints are local comparison tuples in `run_refmac_rfactor_operation`, not a separate normalization helper.
- **Sibling collision aggregation, operational consumers, and early-finalization preservation (`#1273`, `#1278`)**:
  - `_write_json_with_sibling_collision_fallback` (`4223–4835`) aggregates losing collision attempts across `prior_collision_temp_cleanup_statuses`, `prior_collision_retained_temporaries`, `prior_collision_temp_cleanup_failures`, `prior_collision_temp_cleanup_status`, and `sibling_temp_cleanup_status` / `sibling_temp_handling_succeeded` / `sibling_temp_cleanup_succeeded` (propagating callable `payload_factory` `FileExistsError` without retry). Child, parent, fallback-anchor, fallback-directory, and final collector publication paths (`_persist_collector_json_with_fallback`, `_write_to_selected_admitted_directory`, and `_handle_post_write_cleanup_failure_if_any`) enforce `_is_json_temp_handling_satisfied` / `_is_json_temp_status_handling_satisfied`; satisfied temporary handling never erases other collector or persistence failures.
  - On post-readback namespace failure, the writer appends `post_exc` to `eff_causes` before interruptible `_finalize_temp_cleanup` (3666–3671). Actual temporary `OSError` objects are retained and deduplicated through the applicable cause/status and matching-receipt paths; owned directory descriptors are closed by the outer cleanup. This does not promise shared-cause or receipt publication through every interrupt: the readback-error handler calls `_finalize_temp_cleanup` before appending `readback_exc` (3527,3597), so an interrupt there can bypass those later writes; Python exception context is a separate channel. Full `directory_close_failures` lists belong to exception/status metadata and the primary-close receipt; earlier partial receipts receive the corresponding scalar close metadata, not a promised full list on every receipt.
