# Thresholds and standards registry

The single source of truth for every numeric threshold the harness scores against. The per-task
driving examples (`driving_example_T<NN>.md`) and the combined `driving_example.md` cite this file
rather than restating values, so a threshold is defined once and audited once.

Each entry names its **provenance**, which also says how freely it may be changed:

| Tag | Meaning | Change policy |
|---|---|---|
| `[schema]` | A definition committed in `schemas/protstruct_review.yaml` (`ResidueOutlierKind`). | Change only with the schema. |
| `[MolProbity]` | Richardson-lab Top8000 percentile standard. | Tracks the upstream standard. |
| `[literature]` | A published threshold (cited in full below). | Tracks the cited paper. |
| `[catalog]` | A value stated in `ref/catalog.yaml`. | Change with the catalog. |
| `[template]` | A cross-tool **agreement tolerance** (how close two tools must land). | Safe to tune with evidence. |
| `[calibration]` | A sanity check against a known-good case or a deposition. | Safe to tune with evidence. |
| `[benchmark]` | An agreement tolerance **measured in this repo** by running both tools over a test set (audit trail under `ref/research/`, script under `scripts/bench_*.py`). | Change by re-running the benchmark, not by argument. |

`[template]` and `[calibration]` values are engineering tolerances, not scientific claims — tune them
as the harness accumulates evidence. `[schema]`, `[MolProbity]`, and `[literature]` values track
external standards — change them only when the standard does, and update the citation.
`[benchmark]` is the strongest tag available for a cross-tool tolerance: unlike `[template]`, the
magnitude was observed rather than inferred.

## 1. Standard outlier definitions

Committed in `schemas/protstruct_review.yaml::ResidueOutlierKind`; percentile bases are the
Richardson-lab Top8000 reference set (high-resolution ≤ 2.0 Å structures).

| Outlier | Definition | Provenance |
|---|---|---|
| Ramachandran outlier | backbone φ,ψ outside the **99.95th** percentile region | `[schema]` `[MolProbity]` |
| Rotamer outlier | side-chain χ outside the **98th** percentile favored region | `[schema]` `[MolProbity]` |
| Cβ deviation | > **0.25 Å** from ideal | `[schema]` |
| Clash | steric overlap ≥ **0.4 Å** | `[schema]` |
| Bad geometry | bond / angle / planarity > **4σ** | `[schema]` |
| Cis-peptide | non-Pro cis ω | `[schema]` |

> **Reference-set caveat.** Top8000 percentiles were built from ≤ 2.0 Å structures. Applying them to
> a lower-resolution model still computes a percentile, but the outlier bar was set against tighter
> geometry than that model can deliver — note it when a model is well below 2.0 Å.

| Quantity | Threshold | Source |
|---|---|---|
| TM-score fold call | **> 0.5** ≈ same fold; **< 0.17** ≈ random pair | Xu J, Zhang Y. *Bioinformatics* 2010; 26(7):889–895. `[literature]` |
| CC½ resolution-cutoff floor | high-resolution shell is usable down to **CC½ ≈ 0.1–0.2** (significance is sample-size-dependent: CC > 0.3 significant at n > 100, CC > 0.08 at n > 1000 — prefer a per-shell significance test or paired refinement over a fixed floor) | CC½/CC\* definition: Karplus & Diederichs. *Science* 2012; 336(6084):1030–1033. Cutoff value: **Diederichs & Karplus. *Acta Cryst.* D 2013; 69(7):1215–1222** ("Better models by discarding data?"). `[literature]` |
| L-test twinning | untwinned ⟨\|L\|⟩ ≈ **0.5**; perfect twin ≈ **0.375** | Padilla JE, Yeates TO. *Acta Cryst.* D 2003; 59:1124–1130. `[literature]` |
| CAPRI class from DockQ | High **≥ 0.80**; Medium **[0.49, 0.80)**; Acceptable **[0.23, 0.49)**; Incorrect **< 0.23** | Basu S, Wallner B. *PLOS ONE* 2016; 11(8):e0161879. `[literature]` |
| pLDDT confidence cutoff | per-residue **pLDDT ≥ 70** = confident (trim below); ≥ 90 = very high | Jumper J et al. *Nature* 2021; 596:583–589. `[literature]` |
| Phaser MR solution | translation-function **TFZ > 8** = confident/definitely-solved; 5–8 ambiguous | McCoy AJ et al. *J. Appl. Cryst.* 2007; 40:658–674. `[literature]` |
| Real-space density fit (ligand/loop) | **Accuracy: RSZD** significant at **±3σ** (misplaced atoms / unexplained density); **precision: RSZO** floor **~1σ**. | Cryo-EM map resolution (FSC) | gold-standard FSC threshold **0.143** (half-maps); model-map FSC **0.5** | Rosenthal PB, Henderson R. *J. Mol. Biol.* 2003; 333(4):721–745. `[literature]` |

The quantity ranges **2.06–4.35 Å** across the 36 benchmark entries with a recorded crossing (1.87 Å over all 60 including round 23's screened set), so no absolute band serves both ends — one wide enough to cover the data (0.30 Å) would permit an 11 % change on a 2.7 Å measurement. **Caveat — an untested hypothesis resting on n = 2, not a rule: the Δ *may* be least trustworthy when the crossing starts far beyond the map's own resolution.** `d_FSC_model_pre / d_min` exceeds **1.3** for exactly two entries on record — 9H7U (1.372) and 10BU (1.360) — and those are the **two largest excursions in the whole set**, −36.15 % and +4.786 %, in opposite directions. Median |Δ| is **20.5 %** above that ratio against **0.112 %** below it, and the ratio distribution has a clean gap (1.372, 1.360, then 1.076). The correlation over all 36 entries is ρ = +0.346, p = 0.039 — found *against* the arithmetic, since a larger `pre` inflates the outcome's own denominator. **But it is n = 2**: it fails on removing either entry (ρ = +0.288, p = 0.094), the 1.3 split is post-hoc, and 10ME contradicts it (4th-largest excursion, 29th of 36 on the ratio). Round 8's rule applies — a mechanism from two data points is a hypothesis. **Round 23 ran the successor test and could not complete it**: screening 24 fresh entries on that pre-refinement ratio found **zero** above 1.3, which is a one-in-four outcome at the 5.6 % base rate and consistent with it (Fisher p = 0.512). The hypothesis is therefore **untested — neither supported nor refuted** — and needs ~60–90 screened entries to yield the 3 candidates a powered comparison requires. **What round 23 did establish**, over the **60** crossings now measured: median ratio **0.9843** with **50 of 60 (83 %) between 0.73 and 1.01** and a right-skewed tail, so the sustained crossing tracks the map's stated resolution closely in the ordinary case. The outlier fence is **1.074**, not 1.3, and **4 of 60** clear it — one of which, **10EU (ratio 1.076), was already refined and moved only −1.084 %**, just under the 10× bar the hypothesis predicts. So the hypothesis is untested at 1.3 and has one near-miss at 1.074. **Round 40 answered it by measuring the mechanism instead of screening for it (#224).** The *ratio* is 2-point leverage — over the same 19-entry set its Spearman ρ with |excursion| collapses from +0.319 to **+0.049** once 9H7U and 10BU are removed — but **crossing determinacy, measured as how far a fixed 0.1–0.3 Å coordinate perturbation moves the sustained crossing, predicts |excursion| robustly**: ρ = +0.505 over 19, **+0.792 with the extremes removed** (leave-one-out 0.75–0.85), and **partial ρ = +0.773 controlling for cc_mask_pre** — so it is not the #234 fit confound. The large excursions are **real model movement, not estimator jitter**: a controlled perturbation moves the crossing 2–2900× *less* than refinement did (9H7U's 36.15 % excursion against a 0.012 % perturbation shift at σ = 0.2 Å), consistent with 10BU reproducing byte-identically. So the caveat is real but the ratio was the wrong measure of it — treat a crossing whose position is perturbation-sensitive as the one whose Δ will be large, and a large Δ there as a genuine degradation to check. The n = 2 ratio hypothesis is **retired** (`tolerance_benchmark_round40.md`, `scripts/bench_dfsc_determinacy.py`). **Caveat: the range's upper end rests on one entry whose value is not in the per-entry TSV** (9VAM: its row there is `delta-only`, but `ref/research/tolerance_benchmark_round12.md` records the measurement in full — 6.1020 → 6.3629 Å, Δ +0.2609 — so the figure **is** checkable, just not by `check_registry_figures.py`, which reads only the TSV. The largest crossing in the TSV is 4.35 Å, 10BU) — without it an absolute 0.15 Å band would also pass, so the evidence for the *shape* is thinner than for the number. A `|Δd|/d²` band, which the curve's uniform 1/d sampling suggests, was tested and fits *worse* (max/median 15.4 vs 14.0). The three large excursions are genuine curve movement, not estimator jitter: 124, 399 and 117 shells respectively — but note only **one of the three is a degradation** (9VAM); the other two are improvements, so this evidence supports the size of the movement, not a breach count, and that is an **upper bound** — part of each Δ is the sustained estimator's shell quantisation, not model movement (27WR's Δ is ~2 shells) | `[benchmark]` (`ref/research/tolerance_benchmark_refinement_deltas.md`) |

## 6. Negative-control verdict rules (gold-standard starts as negative tests)

Used by the negative-control series (`ref/research/negative_control_benchmark_plan.md`, tracker
#295): a sub-Å gold standard is the *start*, a refinement is the subject, and any change from the
pristine start is a candidate degradation graded by **cross-tool agreement** — never by one tool
family, and never PHENIX grading PHENIX. The machine-readable source for every figure in this
section is `scripts/bench_recover_leg.py` (`REGISTERED_FIT_THRESHOLDS`,
`REGISTERED_FIT_THRESHOLDS_ANIS`, `CANDIDATE_LEG_THIRD_OPINION_STANDDOWN`). The threshold tables are
re-derived **on demand** from the committed round record named in their rows (`fit_thresholds_from_record`,
`anis_thresholds_from_record`, called by the drivers and by validate 3b, each asserting the literal equals
the record); the stand-down set is registered by hand from the round-11 L2 else-branch.
`scripts/check_negative_control_records.py` (validate 3b) asserts this section restates those constants
exactly — the negative-control clause of `CODING_STANDARDS.md` rule 10. Drivers cite this section and
import the constants; they do not restate the numbers.

| Rule | Registered form | Provenance |
|---|---|---|
| Family flags | Four per entry and subject. **F-data**: ΔR-free > +3·S_r2 on **both** registered R paths (`phenix.model_vs_data`, `gemmi_rfactor`), S_r2 = round-2 measured noise scales **0.00275 / 0.00260**; REFMAC sign disagreement is a named cross-tool conflict and the flag stands down. **F-geom**: the §4 geometry clause, d_min < 2.5 Å branch, in its bounded form (favored − 0.5 pp, or rotamer + 4 pp, or the bounded clashscore clause), via the cctbx trio with REFMAC zBOND as confirmation. **F-protected**: ≥ 1 protected-outlier fix. **F-shift**: unmasked Cα shift > **0.12 Å** (§4 ΔRMSD band; conservative toward not flagging on the unmasked-only shift) | `[benchmark]` round 3 B3 (`negative_control_round3_preregistration.md`; S_r2 from `negative_control_round2_screen.json`) |

Two invariants sit above the table. A **falsified prediction stays falsified**: committed rounds are
never re-judged, only disclosed — every falsified prediction in the series is reported as registered
(round-1 P4, round-3 Q2/Q3, round-5 W4, round-6 U3, round-7 V3/V4, round-10 Y2 and round-11 Z3
among them; prediction letters are reused across rounds, so a letter names nothing without its round). And a **threshold changes only by re-registration** from a committed null record, with
the record-vs-registration assertion (`fit_thresholds_from_record`, `anis_thresholds_from_record`)
proving the new table before any verdict uses it.
